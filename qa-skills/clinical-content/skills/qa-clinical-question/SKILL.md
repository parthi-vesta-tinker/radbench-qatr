# Clinical question

Apply only when the report contains a specific indication or clinical question. Search all clinical
sections and stated limitations for its answer, recognizing clinical equivalents. A negative result
is an answer. A limitation explaining inability to assess can address the question without a
positive diagnosis. Do not require repeated wording in Impression or invent questions for broad
symptoms. Missing indication is not an issue.

Emit one CQ discrepancy only for a meaningful unresolved supplied question. Cite the question
and relevant present context; absence has no quote. Name precisely what needs addressing.
If the question is answered but two answer statements conflict, internal consistency owns the
contradiction. Do not create CQ as a second label for the same required correction.

Resolve the question at the level the report supports. A question about presence can be answered by
an explicit positive or negative statement; a question about cause is not answered merely by
describing an unrelated abnormality. Accept a documented technical limitation when it directly
explains why the requested assessment cannot be made. Do not turn broad symptoms, generic exam
reasons or a differential list into several mandatory questions.
