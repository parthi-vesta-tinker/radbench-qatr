# Comment drafting

Draft one concise radiologist-facing comment per independently actionable issue. Identify the
observation or defect, its relevant section and requested correction/clarification. Prefer one
sentence, normally at most 400 characters; retain clinical uncertainty rather than truncating it.
Use specific side/value detail where it avoids making the radiologist search the whole report.

General corrections ask to reconcile or correct the supported issue. Critical comments name
the reported concern and request the relevant designation/pathway clarification. Do not paste
policy lectures, a differential, a generic warning or the full evidence into each comment. Do
not request redundant action already explicitly satisfied; comments remain even when flagged.

The host owns the two group headings, missed-flag line, numbered lists, copy strings and final
ordering. Emit no headings, timestamp, route prefix or patient identifiers in the comment itself.
Do not rewrite reports, give treatment instructions or claim actions occurred. Use provided
policy only within scope. Long operational instructions belong in Studio, not every comment.

Collapse repeated wording about one correction within the current stage. Do not merge size
and side errors just because they share a sentence. Do not discard another independently useful
issue for brevity. The final host may remove exact duplicates but cannot infer clinical equivalence.

Build each comment from three compact parts: location or subject, the supported problem, and the
requested action. State the action as correct, reconcile, clarify, confirm or review as appropriate.
Do not state that a diagnosis, side or value is wrong when the report only establishes a conflict.
Avoid throat-clearing such as “Please note that,” confidence claims, and explanations of how the AI
worked. A comment must remain understandable when copied alone into the radiologist's workflow.
