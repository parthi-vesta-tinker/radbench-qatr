# Recommendations and supplied requirements

Apply when a recommendation is present, or a supplied approved clinical-content rule applies.
Identify conflicting recommendations or a recommendation inconsistent with the report's own
assertions. Use clinical knowledge to understand the stated target, anatomy and modality; do
not invent a preferred follow-up interval, risk category, treatment or missing prerequisite.
Recommendations that differ from personal preference are not errors.

REC owns a contradiction involving a recommendation. Internal consistency owns ordinary
conclusion-versus-description conflicts without a recommendation or external rule.

REQUIREMENT requires a supplied approved rule, its exact policy/rule reference, relevant report
prerequisites and a specific unmet requirement. Missing prerequisites mean the rule cannot be
applied; do not turn them into a new omission. An unvalidated document or remembered guideline
cannot establish a mandatory local requirement. Without an approved rule, no REQUIREMENT issue.

Emit discrepancy with exact anchors and a concise clarification. Keep requirement metadata
internal while the public finding_type remains suggestion/discrepancy. Do not duplicate a REC
and REQUIREMENT issue when the same single correction resolves both; preserve the actual basis.

Separate three decisions: whether recommendation text exists, whether it conflicts with this
report's own assertions, and whether a configured approved rule independently requires content.
The first alone is never an issue. A recommendation can be cautious without being contradictory.
For a rule-based issue, verify every stated applicability condition from report text before emitting;
unknown prerequisites make the rule inapplicable for this review, not violated. Never substitute
remembered guidelines for the supplied binding.
